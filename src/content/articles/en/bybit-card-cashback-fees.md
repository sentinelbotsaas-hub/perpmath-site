"Up to 10% cashback" is the biggest line on the Bybit Card page. I went through Bybit's help-center fee table, the rewards rules and the Asia Pacific cardholder terms page by page, and the same card can net anything from −1.65% to 9.1%. It depends on your tier, on whether the purchase is in US dollars, and on how much you have spent that month. The figures were read from Bybit's public pages on 19 November 2026 and checked against the English pages on 22 November 2026. Only the section "A real statement" uses two purchases from my own account. Everything here is the Asia Pacific card; other card regions have their own rows in the fee table.

## How does Bybit Card cashback work? Six tiers, 2% to 10%, each with a monthly cap

Cashback is paid as points that convert to USDT. Every 1 USD spent earns 10 to 50 points and each point is worth 0.002 USDT, so the rate is 2% to 10%, and every tier has a monthly cap. Your tier comes from your VIP level or from the month's spending, whichever is higher. The table is taken from the help center's [introduction to Bybit Card rewards](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Introduction-to-Bybit-Card-Rewards) (page dated 8 November 2026, checked 22 November 2026).

| Tier | VIP level | Or monthly spend | Points per 1 USD | Rate | Monthly cap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 Base | none | 0 USD | 10 | 2% | 2,500 points, 5 USD |
| Tier 2 Beta | VIP 1, 2 | 500 USD | 10 | 2% | 25,000 points, 50 USD |
| Tier 3 Alpha | VIP 3 | 3,500 USD | 20 | 4% | 75,000 points, 150 USD |
| Tier 4 Apex | VIP 4, PRO 1 | 9,500 USD | 30 | 6% | 125,000 points, 250 USD |
| Tier 5 Omega | VIP 5, PRO 2 | 12,500 USD | 40 | 8% | 200,000 points, 400 USD |
| Tier 6 Infinite | Supreme VIP, PRO 3 to 5 | 25,000 or 25,500 USD | 50 | 10% | 300,000 points, 600 USD |

<!-- unique:cross-reference -->
For the Tier 6 spending threshold I found two numbers. The help-center table says 25,000 USD. Bybit's [rewards program page](https://www.bybit.com/en/pay-rewards/promo/rewards-details/) (no date on the page, checked 22 November 2026) says 25,500 USD. The Chinese versions of the two pages show the same split, and the other five tiers agree. Which figure the system applies can't be told from the public pages, so the calculations below show both.

A few rules feed straight into the amounts. From 10:00 UTC on 5 October 2026, only purchases funded with crypto earn points. Purchases paid from a fiat balance earn none, and what counts is the moment the merchant posts the transaction. A tier unlocked by spending takes effect the day after the qualifying transaction is confirmed and lasts until the end of the following month. Bybit Card and Bybit Pay spending are added together for the threshold and share one point cap.

How long points stay pending is stated two ways: the help center says 4 calendar days, and the rewards page FAQ says typically within 3 calendar days. With auto cashback switched on, points are converted every day into the coin you choose and paid into the Funding Account. The minimum for a conversion is 50 points.

## How much spending fills the cap? Cap ÷ rate

The spend that fills the cap is the monthly cap divided by the rate, and anything above it earns 0. Tier 6's 600 USD cap is full at 6,000 USD.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
For the six tiers that comes to 250, 2,500, 3,750, 4,166.67, 5,000 and 6,000 USD. The Tier 1 cap is the one people overlook. A rate of 2% sounds as if it runs all month, but it stops at 250 USD of spending and every purchase after that earns nothing, unless the month's total reaches 500 USD and lifts you to Tier 2, where the cap grows to 50 USD. The [rewards FAQ](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Bybit-Card-Rewards) (page dated 8 November 2026, checked 22 November 2026) states that once the monthly point limit is reached no more points are earned that month until the tier is upgraded, and that a refund does not give back the cap you used.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
Here is the same thing from the other side. Spend 10,000 USD at Tier 6 and the cashback is still 600 USD, so the rate drops from 10% to 6%. At 25,000 USD it is 2.4%. The 10% holds for the first 6,000 USD and no further.

## Is spending your way up a tier worth it? Cap ÷ threshold, and Tier 6 comes to 2.35% to 2.4%

On the numbers, every tier from 4 upward pays a lower effective rate than Tier 3. If you are not a VIP and keep your tier by spending the threshold each month, the most that month can pay is the cap, and the cap divided by the threshold is your real rate.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
| Tier | Monthly threshold | Most cashback that month | Cap ÷ threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 2 | 500 USD | 10 USD | 2.00% |
| Tier 3 | 3,500 USD | 140 USD | 4.00% |
| Tier 4 | 9,500 USD | 250 USD | 2.63% |
| Tier 5 | 12,500 USD | 400 USD | 3.20% |
| Tier 6 (help center) | 25,000 USD | 600 USD | 2.40% |
| Tier 6 (rewards page) | 25,500 USD | 600 USD | 2.35% |

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
I had assumed a higher tier meant a better rate, and the arithmetic points the other way. At Tier 2 and Tier 3 the threshold is below the spend that fills the cap, so you get the headline 2% and 4%. From Tier 4 up, the threshold is far above that spend, and everything beyond it earns 0. For someone climbing by spending, Tier 3's 4% is the best of the six, and Tier 6's 2.35% to 2.4% is only a little better than Tier 2.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
Even this table flatters the result. A tier takes effect the day after you qualify, so the spending earlier in the qualifying month is paid at the old tier and you collect less than the table shows. The month that pays 10% from start to finish is the next one, as the tier lasts until the end of the following month. Say month one is 25,500 USD to qualify and month two is only the 6,000 USD that fills the cap. The two months together pay at most 1,200 USD on 31,500 USD of spending, which is 3.81%. That is a ceiling, not an expected value.

The 10% really stands in one case only: the tier comes with VIP status and does not have to be bought with spending. The help center's [VIP program article](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Introduction-to-Bybit-VIP-Program) (page dated 23 October 2026, checked 22 November 2026) puts Supreme VIP at a 30-day trading volume of 100 million USD or more in spot, or 500 million USD or more in derivatives.

## What does it cost? 0.9% conversion, plus 2% on anything not in USD

The Asia Pacific card has two percentage fees on purchases: a 0.9% crypto conversion fee whenever crypto funds the payment, and a 2% foreign exchange fee when the transaction currency is not USD. The annual, inactivity and cancellation fees are all 0. These are the Asia Pacific rows of the [fees and limits page](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Fees-and-Spending-Limits-Bybit-Card) (page dated 6 November 2026, checked 22 November 2026).

| Item | Asia Pacific card | When it applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Virtual card issuance | 0 | The page says the fee is waived for now, with no end date |
| Physical card issuance or replacement | 5 USDT, waived for VIPs | When you order the physical card |
| Annual, inactivity and cancellation fees | 0 | Not charged |
| Foreign exchange fee | 2% | Transaction currency differs from the card's currency; added to the card network's rate |
| Crypto conversion fee | 0.9% | Payment funded with crypto; added to Bybit's One-Click Sell rate; minimum conversion 1 USD |
| ATM withdrawal fee | 2% | Only on the part above the first 100 USD each month |

The card's currency is USD, according to the supported-currency table in the [general card FAQ](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Bybit-Card-General-Inquiries) (page dated 3 September 2026, checked 22 November 2026). By the fee table's definition, a purchase in euros, yen or any other local currency is a foreign exchange transaction, and that includes shopping at home when home does not price in dollars. The last step is my inference from the rule. The page's own example is a USD card paying in EUR.

Both fees can land on one transaction. Bybit's [article on how card transactions work](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/How-Bybit-Card-Transactions-Work) (page dated 5 October 2026, checked 22 November 2026) has an example in which a USD card pays 1,260 EUR: the amount is converted at the card network's rate with the foreign exchange fee added, and the part that fiat can't cover is paid from USDT at the One-Click Sell rate plus the conversion fee.

<!-- unique:cross-reference -->
Bybit's own pages state one of these rates in two ways. The fee table and the [Asia Pacific cardholder terms](https://www.bybit.com/en/legal/bybit-card/Bybit-Cardholder-Terms-Conditions-Asia-Pacific) (document dated 16 June 2025, checked 22 November 2026) both give a 2% foreign exchange fee. The FAQ under the "Rest of the World" tab of the [card landing page](https://www.bybit.com/en/cards/) (no date on the page, checked 22 November 2026) says 1%, and says it is added to Mastercard's exchange rate, although the general FAQ lists the Asia Pacific card as a Visa. The physical card fee doesn't match either: it is 5 USDT in the fee table, while the terms say it is quoted according to the delivery location. The calculations here use the 2% on which the fee table and the terms agree.

## The net cashback rate: rate − 0.9% − 2%

The net rate is the cashback minus the two fees, divided by the spend. USD purchases lose 0.9%, and non-USD purchases lose 0.9% plus 2%.

```
cashback = min(spend x rate, monthly cap)
cost     = spend x 0.9% (crypto conversion) + spend x 2% (non-USD purchases only)
net rate = (cashback - cost) / spend
```

The 0.9% can't be dodged, and the reason sits in the rewards rule itself: to earn cashback the purchase has to be funded with crypto, and funding with crypto incurs the conversion fee.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
| Tier | Rate | Net on USD purchases | Net on non-USD purchases | Spend that fills the cap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | 2% | 1.1% | −0.9% | 250 USD |
| Tier 2 | 2% | 1.1% | −0.9% | 2,500 USD |
| Tier 3 | 4% | 3.1% | 1.1% | 3,750 USD |
| Tier 4 | 6% | 5.1% | 3.1% | 4,166.67 USD |
| Tier 5 | 8% | 7.1% | 5.1% | 5,000 USD |
| Tier 6 | 10% | 9.1% | 7.1% | 6,000 USD |

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
The table holds only while spending stays under the cap. Count the 2% foreign exchange fee alone and Tier 6 is 8%; add the fee table's 0.9% and it is 7.1%. Whether the two fees add or compound, Bybit gives no worked example. Compounded they come to 2.918%, a difference of 0.018 percentage points, and I add them throughout.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
Example one: Tier 1, with 400 USD worth of non-USD purchases in the month. Cashback at 2% would be 8 USD, but the cap is 5 USD, so you get 5 USD. The conversion fee is 3.60 USD and the foreign exchange fee 8.00 USD, which makes 11.60 USD of cost. The net is −6.60 USD, a rate of −1.65%. If the same 400 USD were all subscriptions priced in USD, the cost would be 3.60 USD, the net 1.40 USD and the rate 0.35%.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
Example two: Tier 6, non-USD purchases again. Spend 6,000 USD and the cashback is 600 USD, the cost 174 USD and the net 426 USD, a rate of 7.1%. Spend 10,000 USD and the cashback is still 600 USD, the cost 290 USD and the net rate 3.1%. Spend the full 25,500 USD threshold and the 739.50 USD cost is already more than the cashback: the net is −139.50 USD, or −0.55%. With a 25,000 USD threshold the net is −125 USD, or −0.5%. Past 20,689.66 USD of non-USD spending in a month, a Tier 6 cardholder is paying to use the card.

Two exchange-rate spreads are left out, as no public page gives the day's figure: the rate at which the card network turns the local currency into USD, and Bybit's One-Click Sell rate from USDT to USD. The worked example on Bybit's fee page assumes 1 USDT ≈ 0.9995 USD, and that is only an illustration. The real spread shows up on the statement.

Percentage costs like these only show their weight once they are turned into amounts, and the [funding rate article](/learn/what-is-funding-rate/) does the same with a year of funding payments. The [tools page](/tools/) on this site has calculators for perpetual contracts only so far. The card formulas above are short enough to work by hand.

## Which coin is charged, and in what order

Fiat in the card's currency in your Funding Account is charged first, and crypto is sold in the order you have set only when the fiat falls short. The default order is USDT, USDC, GRAM, BTC, ETH, XRP. The general FAQ says you can add coins, remove them or drag them into a new order on the card dashboard.

<!-- unique:cross-reference -->
That order is tied directly to cashback. The Asia Pacific card's fiat currency is USD, so a USD fiat balance in the Funding Account is used first, and fiat-funded purchases have earned nothing from 5 October 2026 on. For a purchase to earn cashback you need to be sure it was charged to crypto.

The card has no balance of its own. Read together, the [introduction to Bybit Card](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Introduction-to-Bybit-Card) (page dated 10 June 2026, checked 22 November 2026) and the general FAQ name the Funding Account and the Flexible Easy Earn balance as where the money comes from. The amount is frozen at authorisation and deducted once the merchant completes the transaction, which can take up to 32 days. BTC or ETH in your payment order is sold at the price at the moment of deduction, so price swings are one more layer of cost. They are the same daily swings measured in the [liquidation article](/learn/what-is-liquidation/).

## What does an ATM withdrawal abroad cost? The first 100 USD a month is free, then 2%

The first 100 USD of ATM withdrawals each month carries no ATM fee, and the part above it is charged 2%. If the cash is not USD, add the 2% foreign exchange fee, and if it is funded from crypto, add 0.9%. ATM withdrawals earn no cashback: the rewards FAQ lists them among the transactions that get no points.

<!-- unique:original-calc -->
For the first withdrawal of the month, taken in local currency and funded from USDT:

| Withdrawal (USD equivalent) | ATM fee | Foreign exchange fee | Conversion fee | Total | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 USD | 0 | 2.00 | 0.90 | 2.90 USD | 2.90% |
| 500 USD | 8.00 | 10.00 | 4.50 | 22.50 USD | 4.50% |
| 1,000 USD | 18.00 | 20.00 | 9.00 | 47.00 USD | 4.70% |
| 3,000 USD | 58.00 | 60.00 | 27.00 | 145.00 USD | 4.83% |

Once the free allowance is used up, every withdrawal costs 4.9%. None of this includes what the local ATM operator charges. Bybit's page warns that some machines add an access fee or offer on screen to convert into the card's currency.

<!-- unique:cross-reference -->
The withdrawal limits come in two versions as well. The fees page gives the Asia Pacific card at limit level 2 up to 3 withdrawals and 3,000 USD within 24 hours, 30 withdrawals and 10,000 USD a month, and 75,000 USD a year. Level 3 has a yearly limit of 100,000 USD, and level 1 has no ATM allowance. The Asia Pacific cardholder terms give 2,000 USD per withdrawal, 5,000 USD a day and 15,000 or 25,000 USD a month. The two documents don't match. Plan with the lower set and treat what the app shows as your real limit.

There are preconditions too. The general FAQ says the Asia Pacific physical card is open only to users at Tier 2 and above, and that a virtual card can only try ATMs that support contactless withdrawals. ATM withdrawals are restricted in Mainland China.

## Can you apply where you live? Nine card regions, and pages that disagree

No public Bybit page I read gives a complete and consistent list of the countries where the card can be issued. The help center describes nine regional card programs, and the application table in the general FAQ names them: Argentina, AIFC, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Australia, Mexico, Brazil, Peru and Asia Pacific. For Asia Pacific the address requirement is a residential address or proof of residence in limited countries within the region, and not one country is named. This is what each official source says about where the card is available.

| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| General FAQ, application table | Nine regions. Asia Pacific means limited countries, none named |
| [Application guide](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/How-to-Apply-for-Bybit-Card) (page dated 10 June 2026, checked 22 November 2026) | The card is available in limited countries only. If the country of residence you select is not supported, your interest is registered and you are told later |
| Asia Pacific cardholder terms, clause 2.3(b) | You must reside in a jurisdiction that the company and its partners treat as eligible. No list is given |

Says the United States, Canada, China, Japan, South Korea, Singapore and Australia are not supported |
| Rewards program terms | The program is not open to users residing in the EEA |

Set side by side, these can't all be right. The structured data lists India, while the landing page itself loads a message that the card is unavailable for India residents. It lists Singapore, Japan, South Korea and Canada, which the wiki article calls unsupported. The wiki says Australia is unsupported, and the help center has an Australia card with its own fee row. Most of the wiki's list is the EEA, and the landing page footnote says the product is not meant for EEA customers. The wiki also calls the card a Mastercard everywhere, where the general FAQ lists the Asia Pacific card as a Visa. The configuration the landing page loaded on 19 November 2026 names nine product regions too, with Colombia where the help center has AIFC, and I can't tell how it bears on applicants.

The one test that settles it is the country selector in the application flow inside the app. If your country of residence is not on it, the application guide says your interest is registered and you are told when the card arrives there.

<!-- unique:cross-reference -->
Being eligible on Bybit's side is one question. Whether the exchange is allowed to serve you where you live is another, and the place to look for that is your own regulator. Taiwan is the case I can document. No Bybit page says in a sentence that residents of Taiwan can or cannot apply, and the statement in the next section comes from a card used there. Taiwan's virtual asset industry association published a [warning](https://twvasp.org.tw/portfolio-item/bybit/) on 11 June 2026 (in Chinese, checked 22 November 2026), reported by the Securities and Futures Bureau: Bybit has not completed anti-money-laundering registration with the Financial Supervisory Commission and may not offer virtual asset services or solicit customers in Taiwan. The bureau's [list of registered businesses](https://www.sfb.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=1053&parentpath=0%2C8) (in Chinese, updated 28 October 2026, checked 22 November 2026) names 11 businesses that completed registration, and Bybit is not one of them. None of this changes the arithmetic above. It may change whether you want to keep money there. This site's relationship with Bybit is set out on the [disclosure page](/disclosure/).

## Limits of these numbers

This article computes the rates written on official pages. It is not a statement. The One-Click Sell rate, the card network's rate and local ATM operator charges are all outside it, and each of them adds cost on top of the figures above.

Whether the two fees add or compound, and whether points are based on the amount before or after fees, is not spelled out on the official pages. The rewards page says only that cashback is applied to the confirmed settlement amount of each transaction, and the rewards FAQ says fees are not eligible for points. I calculated on the basis that the purchase amount earns points and the fees do not; my own two statements show points on the fee too, as the previous section describes. Whether fiat-funded spending counts toward the tier threshold, I could not find either.

Only the Asia Pacific card is computed here. Cards issued under the other regional programs have their own fee rows, card currencies and limits on the same help-center pages, and I have not worked through them. Cards from other issuers are outside this article too.

All of this is the 19 November 2026 version of the rules, checked again on the English pages on 22 November 2026. The last line of the fees page says Bybit may introduce or amend fees and limits according to market conditions, and the rewards rules changed once in October 2026 alone. The contract side of trading costs is covered elsewhere on this site: the [position size calculator](/tools/position-size-calculator/) is one place to start, and the open-source backtester on the [bot page](/bot/) charges fees and slippage on every fill.

## FAQ

### Does the Bybit Card have an annual fee?

No. The fees page lists the annual, inactivity and cancellation fees as none, and the virtual card is free to issue. The Asia Pacific physical card costs 5 USDT, waived for VIPs, while the cardholder terms say the physical card fee is quoted by delivery location.

### Is a purchase in my local currency charged the foreign exchange fee?

Under the fees page's rule, yes, whenever your local currency is not the card's currency. The Asia Pacific card is denominated in USD, and the 2% applies to any transaction in another currency. The official example is a USD card paying in EUR, and the statement in this article shows the fee on a TWD purchase.

### What does it take to get the 10% cashback?

You need Tier 6, Infinite. That means Supreme VIP or PRO 3 to 5, or monthly spending of 25,000 USD (help center) or 25,500 USD (rewards page). The monthly cap is 600 USD, which 6,000 USD of spending fills, and only crypto-funded purchases count.

### Do ATM withdrawals earn cashback?

No. ATM withdrawals earn no points, and neither do fees. Withdrawing 500 USD worth of local currency costs 22.50 USD across the three fees, which is 4.5%, before whatever the local ATM operator charges.

### Which transactions earn no cashback?

The rewards FAQ lists these: fees and charges, cancelled or refunded transactions, ATM withdrawals, top-ups of prepaid cards and e-wallets, remittances and transfers, securities brokers and gambling merchants, and transactions moved in through third-party card services. Purchases paid from a fiat balance have also been excluded from 5 October 2026 on.

### How can I check these numbers myself?

Cashback is the spend times the rate, capped at the monthly cap. The cost is the spend times 0.9%, plus 2% if the purchase is not in USD. The source of every rate, the contradictions between pages and the items I could not find are saved in this site's source code as `research/bybit_card_2026-11-19.json`. I checked the formulas against two TWD purchases on my own account, described under "A real statement". I have no statement yet for an ATM withdrawal or a USD purchase.
